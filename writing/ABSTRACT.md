TL;DR: During reinforcement learning reward hacking is often noticed after it had already affected the policy, in this paper we study how to remove undesired behaviors while preserving the desired capabilities
Abstract:
During reinforcement learning, a misspecified reward term is often identified only after the policy has optimized against it for many updates. At large scale such runs are rarely restarted or rolled back to an earlier checkpoint, because the same training also produced useful capabilities. The installed behavior appears on inputs the erroneous term never scored, and continuing to train under the corrected reward need not remove it. We introduce the task of reward-hack unlearning, the removal of the behavior the erroneous reward installed while retaining what the run learned from the correct one. Unlike in standard or corrective unlearning, the behavior was acquired through RL updates rather than supervised training, and the practitioner typically has access to the full history of the erroneous updates. We compare candidate repairs, including continued training under the corrected reward, behavior cloning from the policy before the error, and an offline replay of the recorded updates with the erroneous advantage negated. We introduce environments in which a reward error installs a hack while the run also produces a measurable capability gain, and we find that the targeted repairs remove the behavior beyond the distribution the reward error applied to, at a small fraction of the cost of retraining.
